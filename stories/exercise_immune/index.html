Regular exercisers' immune cells look readier to fight

A new study in Science Advances compared the blood of 40 regular exercisers and 46 sedentary young adults, cell by cell. In the exercisers, killer T cells and natural killer cells looked readier to act, other immune cells got better at showing invaders to them, and their bodies burned sugar and fat better. It's a link, not proof, from a small group of young, healthy adults.
